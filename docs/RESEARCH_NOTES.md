# Research notes — evidence highlights, context and open questions

**Editorial refresh: 16 October 2026 · source audit reviewed 9 October 2026**

These notes organize the **specific measured findings** in the selected source audit. They do not constitute a new independent systematic review, a medical recommendation or commercial health claims.

## Findings worth understanding

- **Creatine:** A substantial established evidence base concerns repeated high-intensity physical performance. Human cognition research is active: some reviews report specific-domain signals, while others find inconsistent results. These are separate outcomes.
- **Magnesium bisglycinate:** A randomized study in 155 adults reported a small improvement in subjective insomnia severity after four weeks at 250 mg/day *elemental* magnesium. The result is an interesting form-specific signal, not a clinical guarantee.
- **Glycine:** Small trials have reported changes in some subjective sleep and next-day measures with 3 g before bedtime. GlyNAC studies additionally explore the glycine-plus-NAC combination.
- **Taurine:** A placebo-controlled prehypertension trial and a small pooled analysis reported modest reductions in blood-pressure measures at studied gram-scale doses.
- **Vitamin D3, K2, B12, folate and choline:** Established nutritional functions are important. Selected studies investigate deficiency-related care, bone measures, biomarkers and specific pregnancy outcomes. General nutritional roles should not be reduced to the result of a single unrelated prevention trial.
- **NR:** Short-term controlled trials measured higher NAD-related metabolites; that is a concrete pharmacodynamic finding.
- **CoQ10:** Q-SYMBIO reported a positive two-year clinical event result in people with chronic heart failure using CoQ10 alongside standard therapy; the trial population matters.
- **TMG / L-5-MTHF:** Controlled studies observed homocysteine changes at specific doses.
- **PQQ, MK-7, quercetin and other compounds:** Some studies report outcome- or subgroup-specific favorable signals, with evidence quality and replication varying substantially.

## How to interpret uncertainty without blanket verdicts

A *null finding* in a trial tests **that endpoint in that population at that dose**; it does not prove that an ingredient has no biological function. Similarly, a positive biomarker finding is valuable in its own domain but does not establish a clinical or longevity effect. The index retains relevant neutral evidence, including:

- **Creatine cognition:** the preregistered cognition trial reported no significant primary effects, although other research has identified possible domain-specific signals; high-intensity exercise evidence stands independently.
- **Vitamin D3:** VITAL did not reduce the specified cancer/CVD primary outcomes and an analysis did not show broad fracture prevention in the generally healthy studied population; this does **not** negate its established nutritional roles or the management of deficiency.
- **Vitamin B12:** a cognitive meta-analysis in people without overt deficiency found no general enhancement; this does **not** negate treating actual deficiency.
- **Spermidine:** SmartAge did not meet its primary memory endpoint, despite exploratory research interest.
- **NR:** measured rises in NAD-related biomarkers do not alone establish improved healthspan or longevity.
- **Ca-AKG:** animal/mechanistic work and the ABLE study protocol motivate further research; this selected dataset does not contain a completed positive human clinical aging-outcome trial.
- **GlyNAC:** combination findings cannot automatically be assigned to glycine used alone.
- **PQQ:** independent replication, multiple endpoints and correction history warrant checking the original papers.
- **TMG, resveratrol and quercetin:** surrogate changes and pooled outcomes must be distinguished from clinical events or lifespan.

These are **study-specific boundaries, not declarations that the ingredient is ineffective or undesirable**.

## Provenance and independent checks still needed

The source audit flagged a correction notice related to the original PQQ paper and a 2025 corrigendum associated with the 2024 creatine cognition meta-analysis. Verify publisher/Crossmark records independently before revising detailed estimates. **A corrigendum is not automatically a retraction.** A complete independent retraction, funding and adverse-event audit has not been performed.

Known shared cohorts and overlapping reviews are tracked in [data/overlapping_evidence.csv](../data/overlapping_evidence.csv). Neither the number of positive labels nor negative labels is a meta-analytic effect estimate.

## Future research priorities

Independent PQQ replication; Ca-AKG completed human clinical aging outcomes; dose/exposure studies for spermidine; meaningful human clinical outcomes for NR beyond metabolite biomarkers; larger glycine sleep trials; magnesium-salt head-to-head comparisons; replication and better population targeting for creatine cognition; TMG/quercetin clinical outcomes; and longer-duration tolerability/interaction follow-up.

For any conclusion, start with the [study records](../data/studies.csv), the specific endpoint and tested dose. For commercial wording, consult the [EU claims and safety notes](EU_CLAIMS_AND_SAFETY.md) separately.
