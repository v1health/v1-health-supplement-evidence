# Editorial research notes — 2026-10-09

These notes summarize issues identified in the supplied scientific audit. They are **not** a new independent systematic review.

## Strong boundaries

- No indexed intervention has demonstrated human lifespan extension in a randomized trial.
- NR biomarker responses should not be described as clinical reversal of aging.
- Spermidine: negative primary result in SmartAge takes precedence over exploratory endpoints.
- Calcium AKG: current curated entries include a narrative review and ABLE protocol, not a completed positive efficacy trial.
- Glycine plus NAC effects cannot be attributed to glycine monotherapy.
- Choline prenatal cohort follow-up does not establish adult memory enhancement.
- Creatine's 0.35 g/kg acute sleep-deprivation study cannot be generalized to routine 2-5 g/day use.
- PQQ subgroup/multiplicity and product-specific funding questions require scrutiny.
- Quercetin blood-pressure meta-analyses share underlying trials.
- Statin-myalgia CoQ10 trials and heart-failure CoQ10 evidence address different patient populations.

## Corrections / provenance to independently confirm

The source audit flagged a correction notice connected with the original PQQ record and a 2025 corrigendum associated with the original 2024 creatine meta-analysis. Record those citations after independent publisher/Crossmark verification; the presence of a correction is **not automatically a retraction**. A comprehensive retraction/funding audit was not completed.

## Priority research gaps

Independent PQQ replication; completed Ca-AKG human aging efficacy results; dose/exposure studies for spermidine; meaningful clinical outcomes beyond NR biomarkers; larger glycine sleep trials; magnesium-salt head-to-head work; external validation of creatine cognitive effects; clinical event data for TMG/quercetin; long-duration adverse-event and interaction monitoring.

For dose/form-specific comparisons, inspect the CSV source references and study limitations.
